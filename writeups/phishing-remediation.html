SOC Incident Investigation & Response & Remediation

Role: SOC Analyst Severity: High Outcome: Contained & Remediated
Splunk SOAR Microsoft Defender XDR Phishing Email Security Incident Remediation

Overview

A phishing email impersonating an internal employee was sent to a member of the HR team, requesting that their salary payment details be changed to a different bank account. The email included an authentic signature format that is typically only known internally, suggesting the sender had some familiarity with the organization's email signature format. The email was reported by the HR employee who received it and was flagged for investigation as a suspected financial gain.

This writeup covers the investigation process, remediation actions taken, and the outcome, with identifying details generalized to protect confidentiality.

Detection

This incident was flagged via user report through the phishing report button in outlook.

Indicator: The sender's email address used a "gmail[.]com" domain with a string of random characters, rather than the organization's standard internal email format.

Investigation

Steps taken to confirm this was a true positive rather than a false alarm:

  • Verified the sender domain's legitimacy.
    Result: Email sender domain was a gmail[.]com, further investigation was required to confirm authenticity of the email sender.
  • Contacted the employee whose signature appeared in the email to confirm whether the Gmail address belonged to them.
    Result: Investigation confirmed it was not their personal email address.
  • Reviewed email logs in Microsoft Defender to identify any other employees who had received an email from the same sender address, in order to determine the full scope of affected recipients.
    Result: Only 1 recipient received the email.

Tools:
Splunk SOAR Microsoft Defender (Explorer) Abnormal AI

Remediation

After confirming that the gmail address was unrelated to the internal employee, it was categorized as a true-positive incident and the following containment and remediation actions were taken:

Step 1 — Email removal

Deleted the malicious email from the employees mailbox to prevent further engagement.

Step 2 — Blocking Email Sender

Blocked the malicious email sender in Microsoft Defender Tenant Allow/Block List to prevent recurrence.

Step 3 — Inform Employee

Communicated the findings to the affected employee to ensure awareness of the incident, encouraging greater vigilance around their finances going forward.

Step 4 — Improve Detection

After closing the ticket, reported the email to the organization's automated email security platform to ensure similar future attacks are automatically blocked by the system.

Outcome

The incident was fully contained with no confirmed fianacial compromise. Remediation was completed within 10 minutes of detection, and no further phishing attempts using the same infrastructure were observed.

Lessons learned

This incident reinforced the importance of checking sender email authenticity results early in triage, as it significantly speeds up confirming true positives versus false alarms.
